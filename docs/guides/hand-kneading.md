# Hand Kneading Bread Dough

Hand kneading builds dough strength through a repeated **fold, press, and turn** motion. Start with a lean or lightly enriched bread dough that can hold together on the bench. Very wet dough may be easier to strengthen with [folds in the bowl](/guides/folding).

## Before You Start

Mix until no dry flour remains. If the dough is rough and tight, cover it for **10–20 minutes** before kneading; a short rest lets the flour hydrate and makes the motion easier. Use a clean bench with only a light dusting of flour if needed. Keep a bench scraper nearby to lift dough that sticks instead of working a lot of extra flour into it.

## The Fold–Press–Turn Motion

1. **Fold:** Pull the far edge of the dough toward you and fold it over itself.
2. **Press:** Use the heels of your hands to push the folded dough away. Press firmly enough to stretch it, without mashing it flat.
3. **Turn:** Rotate the dough about a quarter turn.
4. **Repeat:** Find a comfortable rhythm. As the dough develops, it should gather more cleanly and feel springier.

For a typical lean dough, start checking after **5–8 minutes** of active kneading. Time is only a starting point: flour, hydration, enrichment, and your pace all change how long it takes.

## If It Sticks or Resists

- **Tacky but workable:** Keep going. A little tackiness is normal.
- **Stuck to the bench:** Lift it with a scraper; dust the surface lightly only as needed. Repeatedly adding flour can change the formula.
- **Tears or springs back hard:** Cover and rest it **5–10 minutes**, then continue gently.
- **Very wet and hard to gather:** Use the [illustrated folding method](/guides/folding) instead of forcing this bench technique.

## How to Tell It Is Ready

The dough should become smoother, more cohesive, and somewhat elastic. Lift a small piece and stretch it gently: it should extend before tearing, with some spring when released. A thin membrane can be a useful check for a well-developed wheat dough, but it is not a pass/fail rule for every flour or recipe. Check the recipe's own instructions before chasing a perfect windowpane.

After kneading, cover the dough and continue with the recipe's bulk fermentation. The [fermentation guide](/guides/fermentation) explains how to judge its rise.
